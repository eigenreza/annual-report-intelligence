# Decision log

Dated notes on the technical choices behind the pipeline, with the measurements that motivated them. All entries are from 2 September 2026, in the order the decisions were made.

## Environment and models

- Python 3.11 for the virtual environment. Python 3.14 was also available, but 3.11 has the safest wheel coverage for faiss-cpu and pdfplumber. Installed versions: openai 3.6.0, faiss 1.15.0, pdfplumber 0.11.10, numpy 2.4.6, tiktoken 0.14.0, pytest 9.1.1.
- Default chat model `gpt-4.1-mini`, which accepts temperature 0 and is inexpensive. It can be overridden with `OPENAI_MODEL` in `.env`. Reasoning models that reject an explicit temperature are called without one. Embeddings use `text-embedding-3-small`.

## Parsing

- Tables are detected on the text layer: a header of years or period labels followed by rows that end in numeric columns, with each value re-serialised next to its column label. pdfplumber's `extract_tables()` returned nothing on the BMW five-year overview page, single-column rows on the Ford key metrics pages and fragments on the Tesla production status table, so it is not used.
- Duplicate text layer removal, first version: words grouped by text and rounded x position, a word 15 to 27 points below another in its group counted as a shifted twin, pages with a twin ratio above 0.2 filtered. Twin characters were removed by object identity rather than position, so that a copy landing on an identical original leaves exactly one survivor.
- Second version: removal moved to character level using the page's measured shift, the median of the word-twin offsets (20.7 points on Ford 2023). Copies that land on another original line interleave into unreadable words at word level but still match one shift up at character level.
- Final version, replacing both: Ford 2023 renders 74 of 75 pages twice, but only about 61 pages are rigid 20.7 point copies. Pages such as 9, 18 and 28 re-typeset the copy at 9.7 point instead of 9.9 point, so lines reflow and no geometric shift matches. The character stream is the reliable signature: the page's opening 60 characters reappear later, and at least 80 percent of the stream after that point matches earlier text. Coverage rather than similarity of the two halves is the criterion because page 34 renders one section twice and another once. A threshold of 0.9 missed two short pages whose once-rendered print footer is a large share of the second run, so 0.8 is used. No page of the other reports is flagged. The only content that is not repeated on a doubled page is that print footer, which is dropped with the copy.
- Table headings are searched up to 12 lines above the header, skipping prose and the rows of a preceding table, because the heading that names a table often sits several lines above it and a segment table serialised without its heading reads like a company total.
- Headings are carried across page breaks and inherited by untitled tables under the same section. This took the count of untitled tables in the Ford 2022 report from 13 to 9 and gave both Automotive segment tables on page 40 their heading.

## Chunking

- Text windows target 400 tokens with a cap of 520 and a two-line overlap, cut at sentence or heading boundaries. Tables stay whole or are split by row groups with the header repeated.
- The embedded text carries a context header with entity, year, company, currency and location. The model sees the raw text.
- Every table row is also indexed as a child chunk made of the table header, its title and the row, which resolves to its parent table at retrieval. A mixed table embeds as a blur, and a single line of it needs to be matchable without showing the model a fragment.

## Reference figures

- The benchmark reference notes were verified against the documents. The BMW Finance N.V. net result is a loss of 394,288 thousand EUR for 2023 and a profit of 403,943 thousand EUR for 2022 (income statement on pages 10 to 13 and narrative on page 25 of the 2023 report, page 26 of the 2022 report). ## Answer layer

- The system prompt includes a corpus overview generated from the registry (file, entity, year, currency, entity note) so that the model can state precisely what the corpus does not contain. No figures are included in the prompt.
- Citations take the form (file name, page N) or (file name, paragraphs A-B). The pipeline parses them and lists the cited chunks under the answer, or all retrieved chunks when the answer cites nothing.
- A single pipeline module holds rewrite, retrieve, answer and conversation memory so that the terminal app and the benchmark share one code path.
- Benchmark verdicts are string checks: expected figure groups, caveat patterns, and real-world figures that are absent from the corpus and must not appear. Every answer is also read.

## Retrieval and answering, by benchmark run

- Initial retrieval: k = 8 for a single company, and max(4, ceil(k / n) + 1) chunks per company for n companies. A mentioned year gave +0.03 to chunks containing that year string, and metric questions gave +0.02 to table chunks. Metric synonyms were appended to the embedded query.
- Run 1: 9 of 14 correct and grounded, no leakage. Question 1 lacked the interest income chunk although declining to give a group figure was right. Question 3 answered with the Automotive segment revenue of 115,894 because the segment table had no title. Question 4 cited in prose. Question 10 named no company and got no BMW chunks. Question 11 missed Ford revenue because the "net sales" synonym pulled sales-volume tables, and it lacked the entity caveat.
- Changes after run 1: "sales" removed from the revenue synonyms, since in these reports it means vehicle units. A question about "which company" or "the companies" without a name routes to every company. Multi-company quota raised to 5. Prompt tightened: parenthetical citation form, prefer consolidated totals and label segment figures, name the document the corpus holds when a figure is missing, entity caveat whenever the year is implied. The citation parser accepts the prose form "page N of file". The check for question 1 accepts any labelled BMW Finance figure, since declining to give a group figure is the correct behaviour.
- Reading run 1 also showed question 6 merging the 2022 "Robotaxi & Others" status into the 2023 answer and question 12 padded with deliveries, workforce and a misread CO2 line. Prompt rules added: current-state questions answer from the most recent report and name its year without merging years, and answers cover only what was asked. The citation parser now also accepts citations joined by "and" inside one parenthesis and a trailing "Sources:" line.
- Run 2: 11 of 14. Question 3 was refused because the key metrics table ranked 21st among Ford chunks, its embedding diluted by a dozen mixed metrics while the Automotive segment table ranked third. Question 11 lost the BMW Group 2021 table to five BMW Finance chunks. Question 6 still merged the 2022 status list despite the prompt rule.
- Changes after run 2: the year bonus replaced by a keyword-overlap bonus (0.08 times the share of the question's content words, cut to six-character prefixes, found in the chunk's embedded text). "Interest income" dropped from the revenue synonyms after it pulled Ford Credit balance sheets above the key metrics table. Table rows indexed as child chunks. Questions with "currently", "latest" or "now" and no year restrict each named company to its most recent report. Pipeline version 3.
- Run 3: 8 of 14, a regression. Diagnosis: dense similarity does not separate numeric rows. For "Ford's revenue for the year 2020" the row "Receivables: 2020 = $0.4" scored 0.716 against 0.654 for "Revenue ($M): 2020 = 127,144", and no bonus on the scale of the score spread could reorder that. Lexical matching on its own floods the result with regional and segment tables that carry the same metric word.
- Final ranking design: dense order and BM25 order fused by reciprocal rank with k = 60. A bonus of 0.02 for rows whose own label names the question's metric, or an alias phrase the registry declares for an entity, such as "interest income" for BMW Finance N.V.; an alias matches only when the whole phrase is in the label, because "interest" alone lights up most lines of a financing report. For company-level questions, tables whose title names a segment or region have their score halved. At most two tables per title per company. k = 12, multi-company quota 6. "Growth" removed from the metric synonyms because it rewarded rows labelled "change". The stopword filter was fixed to judge the full word before cutting it to a prefix. Coverage check: every required figure in the top-k for all twelve questions and both follow-ups, with only the optional BMW Finance net loss on questions 1 and 12 outside.
- Run 4: 11 of 14, no leakage. Question 1 declined to quote the BMW Finance interest income although it was in context, because the prompt only asked for subsidiary figures "if they help". The rule is now prescriptive. Question 9's citation "(file, page 20 and page 21)" was not parsed, so the span now allows page words. Question 11 took Ford's 2021 revenue from an Automotive segment table whose heading was lost to a page break, which is why headings are now carried across page breaks. Pipeline version 4.
- Run 5: 14 of 14 correct and grounded, no leakage, both follow-ups rewritten correctly. Three blemishes remain and are left as they are: answer 10 calls BMW's 2021 profit before tax "net income" in one sentence, answer 10 writes a Ford figure in millions where the table says billions, and answer 7 cites "page 42" from a row label that a two-column layout polluted with a table-of-contents number.
